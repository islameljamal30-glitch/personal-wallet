Budgeting with a blocked account

For students in Germany with a blocked account (Sperrkonto) that pays out a fixed amount once a month.

What makes it tricky

Your blocked account may show a big balance, but almost none of it is yours to spend today. Only the monthly payout reaches your current account, and the whole month has to run on it. Rent, health insurance and your phone usually leave early in the month. Some costs come only a few times a year, like the semester fee. If you look at your current account balance alone, the first week can feel richer than it is.

How much is paid out

The amount is set by the German government and changes from time to time. At the time of writing, the amount for students is €992 a month (€11,904 a year). It does change, so check the page of the German embassy or consulate handling your visa for the amount that applies to you. When the payout arrives depends on your blocked account provider.

A simple monthly plan

  1. Start from what has actually arrived. Count the payout once it is in your current account, not before. Money still in the blocked account isn’t spending money yet.
  2. Take out the fixed bills still to pay this month. Rent, health insurance, phone, transport if it isn’t covered by your semester ticket.
  3. Set aside a little for costs that come later. Divide each one by the months until it’s due. A semester fee due in six months becomes one sixth each month.
  4. Keep a small buffer for surprises, if you can.
  5. What’s left is safe to spend on food, going out and everything else this month.

A worked example

Example figures only. Your rent, insurance and fees will be different.

Example monthly budget with a blocked account payout
Payout arrived in your current account€992.00
Rent− €450.00
Health insurance− €140.00
Phone− €15.00
Set aside for the semester fee (€300 in six months)− €50.00
Buffer− €30.00
Safe to spend this month€307.00

€307 for a month is roughly €70 a week. Knowing that on the 1st is much easier than finding out on the 25th.

Tips

  • Your first payout may come a while after you arrive. Plan the first weeks, like the rental deposit, with money outside the blocked account.
  • If you earn money from a side job, count it once it’s paid, not when you expect it.
  • A quick check once a week is often enough. You don’t need to watch every euro every day.
