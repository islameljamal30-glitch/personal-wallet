Other banks: download your transactions as a CSV file

The rules below are those of Personal Wallet’s file reader, as of 19 November 2026.

Finding the export

Most banks put it in the same place:

  1. Log in to online banking, in a web browser or in your bank’s app.
  2. Open Transactions (in German often Umsätze).
  3. Choose Export or Download, then CSV.

If your bank only offers PDF statements, Personal Wallet can’t read those. You can still add entries by hand.

What the file needs

  • One line per transaction, with the column names in a line at the top.
  • A date, like 02.11.2026, 2026-11-02, 2026-11-02 10:23 or 02/11/2026. If a date with slashes could mean two days, Personal Wallet asks which number is the day.
  • An amount: either one column with a minus for money out, or separate money-out and money-in columns. Both 1.234,56 and 1,234.56 work. If it’s unclear, Personal Wallet asks.
  • A description or a name.
  • Columns separated by semicolons, commas or tabs. Up to 5 MB and 5,000 transactions per file.

When Personal Wallet can’t tell which column is which, it shows you the columns with examples and asks you to pick the date, the amount and the description.

What it can’t read

  • PDF statements, and formats such as MT940, CAMT XML or OFX.
  • Lines in a different currency than the account. They’re shown so you can see them, but not added.

If your bank’s file has a status column, payments it marks as declined, reversed or still pending are left out, and Personal Wallet tells you how many.

Bringing it in

In Personal Wallet, choose Bring in your bank’s transactions and pick the file. It’s read on your iPhone only, with no bank login. You check every line, choose categories, and nothing is added until you confirm. Your first import is free. More imports come with Personal Wallet Pro.
